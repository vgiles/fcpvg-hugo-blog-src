I recently watched a video on youtube by the GM/creator Ginny Di about creating a D&D one-shot in an hour. The video resonated with me for a couple of reasons: first, it was sensible and clear, which is always nice. Second, the strategy of using time-limits for creative purposes is one I’ve used a lot in my creative and teaching work over the years; creating within limitations more broadly[1][2] and of course creating something in a time-restrained way in an effort to ‘get out of your own way’ in the creative process. But prior to watching the video, for whatever reason I’d not connected it to creating TTRPG adventures/modules, despite my prep being fairly minimal except when I need to think about the longer trajectory of a campaign, which happens maybe every five or six sessions (depending on how the sessions go). So I wanted to give it a shot. This is what I did and my reflections on the process and outcome, and this post is a kind of companion piece to a reel I posted on Instagram (linky). 

CW: This is obviously political in its story and includes themes of displacement, slavery, and vengeance.

### Finding a monster

The first thing I did (and my first deviation from Ginny’s process) was to peruse the 2024 Monster Manual and find something that I found interesting. I settled on a revenant, a vengeful spirit that occupies new bodies each time its body is destroyed, until a certain spell is cast on the body. Cool: undead are always pretty compelling to me, and vengeful ones? Neat. What I particularly like is the moral ambiguity, but also the possible real-world contexts that can be drawn on. So the question becomes what is its motivation and reason for vengeance, and on whom?’ which of course needs the question of what is the setting? to be answered first. The first problem with this antagonist is that it’s not part of the SRD, so if I ever wanted to turn this into a publishable adventure, I would need to change that or find something else. This isn’t so much of a problem though, as it encourages a creative approach to antagonist creation, and abilities, and that really helps with things like motivations. But for now, as I’m only likely to run this for my table(s), a revenant!

### Setting

I generally try and keep D&D 5.5 to its high-fantasy superhero context, so I decided that a small but rapidly expanding village (on the cusp of becoming a town) about half a day’s ride from the nearest large town. It was started around three years ago, and its primary growth and expansion has been the abundant resources found in the surrounding forest, including good hardwood for building work, animal pelts, and farming potential. The town has an established hierarchy of residents, including an acting and actual mayor, councillors, a well-established inn, market to buy and sell goods, and other things that one would expect from such a place. Ever since the town started there have been occasional disappearances, but people just assumed it was the flow of a town being built: people come and go, right? But disappearances have increased in the last month, and so the council requested a help-wanted sign be placed in the town half a day away looking for adventurers willing to look into it. Upon arrival, the adventurers learn that the mayor has locked themself in their home as they believe they have less than 24 hours to live due to a curse being placed on them.

### Antagonist motivation/background

Even though the history of the town is recent, its formation was one that involved displacing other residents and murdering those who stayed. This was done quickly and brutally to a small clan of goblins who were resident just inside the forest. Those who survived or capitulated were placed into indentured servitude for the mayoral manor, including its construction. The revenant is therefore one of goblins who was murdered, but two siblings are still in indentured service to the mayor’s manor. They are seeking vengeance on all those who perpetuated the violence and displacement, and to rescue or release their siblings and see them to safety. 

### The adventure and current status

This is all about the context, and I only got so far in this, and did not finish setting DCs and other related mechanical features. But here is a list of the points of interest as it currently exists:

* Tharrow's Horseshoe (Inn, fairly busy, some travellers and some locals out to unwind after a day of labour). A slight air of fear hangs on the place though. 

* The Mayoral Manor (where the Mayor and Deputy Mayor and their families reside.)

* Burnt stonework at the edge of town (the remnants of some previous village)

* Local market (a place where local vendors deal their goods, includes an alchemist who appears to know a little arcane lore)

* Forest edge (where the town gives way to forest, many hewn trees are left)

* The forest proper (the light of the world gives way to shadows quickly in this dense forest. Spookywooky)

* Town square (has a community notice board, mostly full of missing persons notices)

#### Combat Encounter 1

* Revenant will attack the adventurers but mostly so it can escape the town for its hiding place in the forest, seeing them as a threat to its objective. If at any point the revenant drops below 70% HP it will flee on its next turn. 

* In the off chance the party defeats it on the first interaction, it will return the following day in a larger body. 

* On the body they will find a drawing of two goblins, both of whom are in service to the Mayoral Manor.

#### Final Encounter

Two options depending on the course of actions:

1. Combat encounter with the revenent, noting that in order to truly save the town, not only does its body need to be killed, but it has to have a specific spell cast on it, otherwise it will re-spawn somewhere else and eventually find its way back. If the PCs have identified it, they will know that, and they will also know whether they can or cannot complete the quest this way. Recommend a Cleric be part of the party.

2. Social encounter with the councillors to let them know it's a revenent, and that it will keep coming unless they make amends. This would follow from some kind of social encounter with the revenant, logically. 

Of course, this may not be how things pan out. 

### Reflections

Whilst I took a break in the middle of doing this to take care of some life things, I dedicated exactly one hour to the task and was pleasantly surprised with how much I could come up with in that time. Ginny Di points out that in a one-shot, a degree of ‘railroading’ is a good thing, so this is developed with that in mind. Previous adventures that I’ve written to DM have been more open than this, but have also been designed to run over multiple sessions, whereas one-shots I’ve always used other peoples’. 

I’ve not included the relevant clues and DCs that I did get to writing in this post, but have done a few, and I plan to keep developing the adventure and then running it. I’d like to try and condense it all into a trifold brochure style (common in games like Mothership), to see how condensed I can present the information, including a map of the area that can double as a battle map. 

This development process though has been really liberating. Stuff doesn’t need to be perfect. Iteration is key (again, something I teach so often but forget to apply to myself!), and the best way to see if it works is to run it, which I have not yet done.
